# Buyer and Practitioner Guide

## What This Layer Helps You See

The Invisible Middle Intelligence Layer helps identify where a system appears to work while people quietly carry the cost of making it work.

It is designed for human-facing systems where completion can look successful while burden is being transferred into people.

## Thirty-Second Explanation

Invisible Middle detects hidden human compensation.

It looks at workflows, platforms, AI tools, forms, portals, dashboards, handoffs, and organisational processes to identify where people are being asked to carry missing structure.

It helps answer:

> Is this system genuinely supporting people, or merely making their compensation less visible?

## Who This Is For

### Education / SEN

Support systems can look organised while children, families, teachers, and SENCOs carry repeated explanation, context repair, emotional containment, and authority burden.

### NDPP / Organisational Design

Operational systems can appear predictable while people carry hidden coordination, interpretation, monitoring, trust repair, and closure burden.

### EdTech and Digital Platforms

Platforms can improve visible access while weakening context continuity, participation, ownership clarity, or cognitive sustainability.

### Workforce Systems and CRMs

Dashboards can show activity while employees maintain unofficial tracking, repeated checking, duplicate validation, and workflow repair behind the scenes.

### AI-Supported Workflows

AI can produce outputs faster while increasing human verification, correction, confidence interpretation, authority burden, and rework.

### Public Services and Care Systems

Forms and portals can complete transactions while people carry context reconstruction, repeated proof, participation loss, and trust-recovery burden.

## Discovery Questions

Use these questions to find the Invisible Middle in a system:

1. Where does the system say the task is complete, but people still have to verify, repair, translate, chase, or explain?
2. Who carries responsibility without real control?
3. What context gets lost when people are compressed into forms, prompts, tickets, or categories?
4. Which users repeatedly check, regenerate, escalate, avoid, or work around the system?
5. Where does trust have to be manually repaired?
6. Where does closure remain unclear after apparent completion?
7. What would need to change before people describe the system as smooth, trustworthy, and sustainable?

## What Buyers Receive

### Invisible Middle Field Map

Shows where burden appears across roles, steps, handoffs, and system conditions.

### Burden / Flourishing Envelope

Structured evidence of cognitive, interpretive, verification, authority, context, closure, trust-repair, participation, and flourishing impact.

### Outcome Classification

Possible findings include:

- SMOOTH
- FRICTION
- DISPLACEMENT
- AUTHORITY SHIFT
- CONTEXT COLLAPSE
- RECHECK LOOP
- TRUST DAMAGE
- PARTICIPATION LOSS
- REBALANCE REQUIRED
- FLOURISHING SUPPORT
- UNFIT

### Rebalance Recommendations

Buyer-readable changes that reduce hidden burden and restore healthier containment.

### Dashboard Snapshot

A visible surface showing structural conditions, burden carriers, evidence strength, and response guidance.

## What This Is Not

This is not a generic AI audit.

The question is not whether work is faster. The question is whether burden moved into people.

This is not UX polish.

Smoother screens do not prove smoother human consequence.

This is not employee surveillance.

The target is not the person showing strain. The target is the missing structure requiring that person to carry compensation.

This is not wellbeing branding.

The layer produces structured interpretation, evidence, and rebalance recommendations.

## Simple Pilot Shape

A first pilot can stay bounded:

```text
One workflow
One user group
One signal family
One burden map
One report
One rebalance recommendation set
```

No protected runtime internals are required for a field-facing pilot.

## Buyer Call Close

A useful closing line:

> We do not need to assess the entire organisation first. Start with one workflow where the system appears complete but people still carry afterwork. The goal is to determine whether the system genuinely supports people, or merely makes human compensation less visible.
