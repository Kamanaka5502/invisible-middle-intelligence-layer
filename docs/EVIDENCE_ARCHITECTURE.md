# Evidence Architecture

## Purpose

Structural interpretation must remain operationally defensible.

The Invisible Middle Intelligence Layer does not interpret signals impressionistically. It requires an evidence architecture that explains why a structural condition is being inferred.

Evidence answers:

> Why does the intelligence layer believe this structural condition is forming?

This creates transparency, interpretive accountability, and commercial credibility.

## Evidence Source Types

### Behavioural Evidence

Observed interaction patterns.

Examples:

- repeated checking
- navigation looping
- hesitation before completion
- repeated reopening
- task abandonment
- duplicated validation
- avoidance
- switching outside the official pathway

### Workflow Evidence

Operational pathway instability.

Examples:

- unresolved handoffs
- duplicated intervention
- delayed escalation
- repeated reassessment
- informal workaround creation
- parallel tracking
- unresolved completion states
- transition ambiguity

### Communication Evidence

Meaning instability expressed through human communication.

Examples:

- repeated clarification requests
- reassurance seeking
- ownership uncertainty
- repeated context explanation
- defensive documentation
- explanation fatigue
- uncertainty about next steps

### System Evidence

Signals generated by platform, process, workflow, or architecture.

Examples:

- alert frequency
- unresolved status states
- workflow interruption
- unstable completion signalling
- repeated system errors
- contradictory outputs
- unclear thresholds

### AI Evidence

AI-specific burden indicators.

Examples:

- repeated override
- confidence uncertainty
- human verification dependency
- ignored recommendations
- unexplained rejection patterns
- re-prompting loops
- output rewrite burden
- AI-generated content requiring unsupported human authority

### Human-Reported Evidence

Structured qualitative evidence from people using, carrying, repairing, or working around the system.

Examples:

- staff reporting uncertainty
- user-reported friction
- organisational observations
- hidden burden disclosures
- reports of overload
- reports of trust loss
- reports of repeated explanation or context reconstruction

## Temporal Pattern Logic

The Invisible Middle rarely becomes visible through isolated moments alone.

It more often emerges through behavioural, workflow, communication, AI, or system patterns developing over time.

The intelligence layer examines:

- recurrence
- persistence
- acceleration
- failed recovery
- repeated reappearance after intervention
- burden accumulation trajectories
- escalation pattern development
- widening participation loss

Time changes interpretive meaning.

A single instance of repeated checking may be informational.

Repeated checking increasing over time may indicate trust instability, unresolved closure failure, predictability degradation, or weakening containment.

A delayed intervention once may be situational.

Repeated delayed intervention across comparable cases may indicate threshold ambiguity, ownership instability, or governance weakness.

Structural interpretation depends not only on what appears, but how patterns evolve.

## Evidence Strength Model

| Strength | Meaning | Operating Use |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | Single weak indicator | Observe only |
| Emerging | Multiple aligned weak indicators | Early structural interpretation possible |
| Moderate | Repeated aligned indicators across time or domains | Operational attention warranted |
| Strong | Persistent multi-domain evidence | Response guidance appropriate |
| Critical | Persistent structural pattern with measurable operational consequence | Redesign, governance response, or optional boundary handoff may be required |

## Evidence Confidence Logic

The same signal increases in confidence when it is:

- repeated over time
- appearing across multiple domains
- corroborated by workflow evidence
- aligned with human-reported experience
- associated with measurable operational impact
- coherent with the surrounding system context

Confidence should strengthen structurally, not emotionally.

## Evidence Envelope

A structured evidence envelope should include:

```json
{
  "case_id": "IM-EXAMPLE-001",
  "signal_family": "workflow",
  "observed_signal": "repeated unresolved handoff",
  "context": {
    "workflow_condition": "unclear ownership after submission",
    "threshold_condition": "unclear escalation criteria",
    "closure_condition": "completion not confidently recognised"
  },
  "evidence_sources": [
    "workflow_evidence",
    "communication_evidence",
    "human_reported_evidence"
  ],
  "temporal_pattern": "persistent across review cycles",
  "evidence_strength": "strong",
  "inferred_structural_condition": "ownership_drift",
  "confidence_basis": "Repeated handoff uncertainty, clarification requests, and unresolved completion states across multiple cases.",
  "recommended_response": "ownership clarification and closure strengthening"
}
```

## Safeguard

Evidence must support structural interpretation without converting the layer into surveillance or behavioural blame.

The target is missing structure, not individual deficit.
